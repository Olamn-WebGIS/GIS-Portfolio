FUTA Nav - Smart Campus Navigator

A student-first campus navigation Web GIS that helps FUTA students and visitors search for mapped places in familiar language, choose a starting point, and preview a walking route.

01 / The challenge

Finding your way around a large campus

New students and visitors may know a destination by a local description like “where they do GST,” not its official building name—and can be left relying on verbal directions to find it.

FUTA NAV brings mapped campus locations and walking routes together in one app. Search by an official place name or a familiar student description such as “where they do GST,” choose a starting point or use your device location, and view the route on an interactive map.

The “Find my route” flow shows the destination, its photo, and an estimated walking time. Offline access helps make the navigator useful when connectivity is limited.

02 / The experience

From asking around to exploring the map

Finding a place without FUTA NAV

  • Ask multiple people for directions to an unfamiliar place.
  • Local names can be confusing when they differ from formal names.
  • Guess the route and travel time without a mapped preview.

Finding a place with FUTA NAV

  • Search for a destination by its local or formal name.
  • Set a starting building or optionally use device location.
  • View a suggested route, estimated distance and walking time, and destination photo.

Project outcome: The web app brings campus place search, mapped location details, and route previews together in one interface. This describes the built features, not a measured reduction in wayfinding time.

03 / Results and impact

Making campus wayfinding easier to approach

Less time spent searching

Designed to help people find a destination faster by bringing place search and route previews together; time savings have not been formally measured.

Support for first-time visitors

Mapped places, destination photos, and route previews give newcomers more context for finding their way around campus.

Discover places without relying only on directions

Search campus locations and preview a route instead of depending solely on asking others where to go.

Useful with limited connectivity

Offline access helps people use the campus guide when internet connectivity is unreliable.

These are intended practical benefits of the app’s features, not results from a formal user study.

06 / Workflow

From field mapping to a route preview

STEP 01

Map campus places

Visited locations, recorded building coordinates, and collected photographs using Mergin Maps.

STEP 02

Prepare spatial layers

Combined field-collected places with OpenStreetMap roads and a digitized campus boundary in QGIS.

STEP 03

Analyze the network

Repaired geometries, clipped data to the campus boundary, and snapped building points to the road network before running shortest-path analysis.

STEP 04

Build the web navigator

Connected campus-place search, starting-point selection, device location, and route previews in a Leaflet web app.

  1. Field data collectionBuildings, coordinates, and photos in Mergin Maps
  2. QGIS processingPrepare campus places, roads, and boundary
  3. Network analysisClean and connect paths for shortest-path routing
  4. GeoJSONPrepare spatial layers for the web map
  5. Leaflet web appSearch locations and preview walking routes
My role: I led the project from field data acquisition through delivery, including geospatial data preparation, network analysis and route modeling, interface design, and frontend development of the web navigator.

07 / Scope and next steps

A useful prototype, with routes to verify

FUTA NAV brings campus data and route previews into one accessible interface. Its usefulness depends on the coverage and connectivity of mapped paths; OpenStreetMap may not include every campus path, so route suggestions should be checked against campus signs and local conditions.

Next steps: Field-check path connectivity and route accuracy, update missing or incorrect paths, and test the navigator with students and visitors.
