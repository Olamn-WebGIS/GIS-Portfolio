FUTA Nav - Smart Campus Navigator

A student-first campus guide that helps FUTA students and visitors search for mapped places in familiar language, choose a starting point, and find a walking route—even offline.

01 / The challenge

Finding your way around a large campus

New students and visitors can struggle to find lecture halls, offices, and other places on campus—especially when local names differ from formal building names.

FUTA NAV brings mapped campus locations and walking routes together in one app. Search by an official place name or a familiar student description such as “where they do GST,” choose a starting point or use your device location, and view the route on an interactive map.

The “Find my route” flow shows the destination, its photo, and an estimated walking time. Offline access helps make the navigator useful when connectivity is limited.

03 / Workflow

From field mapping to a route preview

STEP 01

Map campus places

Visited locations, recorded building coordinates, and collected photographs using Mergin Maps.

STEP 02

Prepare spatial layers

Combined field-collected places with OpenStreetMap roads and a digitized campus boundary in QGIS.

STEP 03

Analyze the network

Repaired geometries, clipped data to the campus boundary, and snapped building points to the road network before running shortest-path analysis.

STEP 04

Build the web navigator

Connected campus-place search, starting-point selection, device location, and route previews in a Leaflet web app.

My role: Solo project — field data collection, road-network cleaning, GIS and shortest-path analysis, and frontend development.

04 / Scope and next steps

A useful prototype, with routes to verify

FUTA NAV brings campus data and route previews into one accessible interface. Its usefulness depends on the coverage and connectivity of mapped paths; OpenStreetMap may not include every campus path, so route suggestions should be checked against campus signs and local conditions.

Next steps: Field-check path connectivity and route accuracy, update missing or incorrect paths, and test the navigator with students and visitors.
